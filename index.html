1 · Foundation & FrameworkQuestion 1 of 10

“How will you audit our current products to find what needs fixing?”

Artera’s problem

Every report and screen was built for the moment it was needed. Nobody holds the full inventory.

My solution

  1. Inventory every surface. Reports, portal, lab-facing, Figma, code.
  2. Let the code answer first. Claude Code lists every component and one-off in days.
  3. Watch the work, then score it. Harm, frequency, rework, commercial weight.

Thirty daysA map of duplication and risk, and the first win.

1 · Foundation & FrameworkProof · Onix case study ↗

Three tiers. One source.
Clinical meaning in schemas, not paint.

Artera’s problem

A “positive” biomarker cannot be just a green.

The framework

  1. Primitive. Raw values. No opinions.
  2. Semantic. Intent, not colour.
  3. Clinical schema, not tokens. Meaning owned with science, rendered through the system.
  4. Theme layers. Brand, product, locale. One source, every platform.
Primitive blue / 600→Semantic action / primary→Component button / background

At Onix, Anvil’s tokens ran the app, the sites, and Storybook from one source.

1 · Foundation & FrameworkQuestion 3 of 10

“How will you ensure accessibility and company-wide understanding of the design system?”

Artera’s problem

Reports get printed, faxed, and read under regulatory scrutiny. The people who need the system are not only designers.

My solution

  1. WCAG 2.1 AA is the floor. Aligned to FDA human factors.
  2. Risk is never colour alone.
  3. One documentation site everyone reads. Storybook as the living reference.

2 · Collaboration & GovernanceQuestion 5 of 10

“What does your governance model look like?”

Artera’s problem

Nobody owns cross-cutting decisions. Clinical claims need a review path that exists.

My solution

  1. A five-person council. Design, engineering, product, science, commercial.
  2. A contribution path. Propose, review, pilot, release.
  3. Three tiers of change. Patch ships. Minor to council. Major adds science sign-off.

2 · Collaboration & GovernanceQuestion 6 of 10

“How will you handle disagreements between teams regarding system updates?”

Artera’s problem

Science wants nuance. Commercial wants simplicity. Engineering wants reuse. All three are right.

My solution

  1. Patient safety and clinical accuracy
  2. Regulatory and privacy
  3. Accessibility
  4. Consistency and reuse
  5. Velocity
  6. Preference

Higher wins. Missing evidence gets a time-boxed pilot, not a longer meeting.

3 · Adoption & ScalingQuestion 7 of 10

“How will you convince product teams to stop using their old workflows and adopt the new design system?”

Artera’s problem

The old way feels faster because it is familiar. Nobody funds migration.

My solution

  1. Earn the first team. The bladder report, shipped faster on the system.
  2. Make it the fastest path. Examples, office hours, AI-assisted migration.
  3. Fund it inside product work. After version one, no new one-offs.

“How will the system scale as the company grows and launches new products?”

4 · Measuring SuccessQuestion 9 of 10

“What key performance indicators or metrics will you track to prove the design system is working?”

Artera’s problem

Without a baseline, every improvement is an anecdote.

My solution

Baseline first, targets second. Six numbers, the same way every quarter.

Delivery

Lead time for a comparable change

Less repeated effort

Adoption

Share of eligible work on shared patterns

Reuse that actually happens

Quality

Design-attributed defects per release

The number engineering stops arguing about

Comprehension

Clinician time-to-answer on the real report

Can a rad onc find the ST-ADT result in ten seconds?

Consistency

Variant count per component

Fewer blues, fewer ways to say risk

Commercial

Lab onboarding time from license to first report

How often sales has to explain the page
4 · Measuring SuccessQuestion 10 of 10

“How will you communicate the return on investment to executive leadership to secure long-term funding?”

Artera’s problem

A design system is invisible when it works.

My solution

  1. One page, quarterly. Shipped, used, improved, next.
  2. Effort into cost. Hours returned, assumptions visible, maintenance included.
  3. Fund in phases. Each quarter earned by the last.
